Technical SEO Audit Checklist for Single-Author WordPress Blogs

Running a single-author WordPress blog means you wear every hat: writer, editor, publisher, and webmaster. When organic traffic dips or fails to grow, the culprit is often technical SEO—the hidden mechanics that help search engines crawl, index, and rank your content.

Because you don't have an engineering team to lean on, you need a streamlined, practical audit process. Here is a no-nonsense technical SEO checklist tailored specifically for solo WordPress bloggers.

1. Fix Indexing and Crawlability Issues

Before worrying about keywords, ensure Google can actually see your site.

  • Check Google Search Console (GSC): Look at the "Pages" report. Are good posts showing up as "Crawled - currently not indexed" or "Discovered - currently not indexed"? If so, your internal linking needs work.
  • Verify your `robots.txt` file: Go to `yourdomain.com/robots.txt` and make sure you aren't accidentally blocking search bots.
  • Submit your XML Sitemap: Use an SEO plugin (like Rank Math or Yoast) to generate a clean sitemap, then submit the URL to GSC.

2. Optimize Site Speed (Core Web Vitals)

Slow sites frustrate readers and hurt rankings. As a solo blogger, you likely rely on shared hosting and plugins, which can bloat your code.

  • Run PageSpeed Insights: Test your homepage and a few recent blog posts. Pay close attention to Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS).
  • Audit Your Plugins: Deactivate and delete plugins you aren't actively using. Replace heavy plugins with lighter alternatives (e.g., swapping a bulky social sharing plugin for a lightweight script).
  • Implement Caching: Install a reliable caching plugin (like WP Rocket or LiteSpeed Cache) to serve static versions of your pages.
  • Compress Images: Never upload raw photos straight from your camera. Use a compression plugin to automatically convert images to next-gen formats like WebP.

3. Clean Up Internal Architecture

As a single author, it’s easy to publish content haphazardly over the years without a structural plan.

  • Fix Broken Links: Broken links waste crawl budget and ruin user experience. Use a free tool like the "Broken Link Checker" plugin (activate, scan, fix, then deactivate) to clean them up.
  • Review Category and Tag Pages: Are you accidentally indexing dozens of empty tag archives? Set thin or empty archives to `noindex` in your SEO plugin settings to prevent duplicate content issues.
  • Implement HTTPS: Ensure your site forces SSL (`https://`). Mixed content warnings (where secure pages load insecure images) will break trust and hurt performance.

Running this audit twice a year keeps your WordPress blog healthy, ensures search engines can easily navigate your archives, and lays a solid technical foundation for your writing.
