Technical SEO Audit Checklist for Single-Author WordPress Blogs

Running a single-author WordPress blog means you wear every hat: writer, editor, and webmaster. When organic traffic plateaus, the culprit is often technical SEO—the invisible foundation of your site.

You don’t need an enterprise-level budget or a development team to fix this. Here is a practical, no-nonsense technical SEO audit checklist designed specifically for solo WordPress creators.

1. Master Your Indexing Settings

Before diving deep, ensure search engines can actually see your best work.

  • Check Visibility: Go to *Settings > Reading* in your WordPress dashboard and confirm that "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" is unchecked. It sounds basic, but it happens more often than you think.
  • Review Your Sitemap: Install a reputable SEO plugin (like Rank Math or Yoast) and verify your XML sitemap is active. Submit this sitemap to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.

2. Streamline Your Plugins

Solo bloggers often fall into the trap of installing too many plugins for minor features.

  • Audit for Bloat: Deactivate and delete plugins you no longer use. Every plugin adds HTTP requests and potential database overhead, slowing down your time-to-first-byte (TTFB).
  • Test Speed: Run your core URLs through Google PageSpeed Insights. Focus on improving Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) by compressing images and choosing a lightweight, performance-focused WordPress theme.

3. Fix Broken Links and Redirects

As your blog grows over the years, old content gets moved or deleted. Broken links frustrate readers and waste crawl budget.

  • Scan for 404s: Use a tool like Screaming Frog (free for up to 500 URLs) or a WordPress broken link checker plugin to find dead internal links.
  • Set Up Redirects: If you delete an old post, use a 3D redirect to send visitors (and link equity) to a relevant, newer piece of content instead of leaving a dead 404 page.

4. Optimize Mobile Usability

Most single-author blogs get the majority of their traffic from mobile devices.

  • Test Responsiveness: Use Google’s Mobile-Friendly Test to ensure text isn’t too small to read and tap targets (links and buttons) aren’t packed too closely together.
  • Check Pop-ups: If you use email capture forms, ensure they don't cover the main content on mobile devices, which triggers Google’s intrusive interstitial penalty.

Final Thoughts

A technical SEO audit isn’t a one-time event; schedule a quick check every six months. By keeping your WordPress backend lean, your sitemap clean, and your mobile experience smooth, you give your content the best possible chance to rank well and serve your readers.
