Technical SEO Audit Checklist for Single-Author WordPress Blogs

Running a single-author WordPress blog means you wear every hat: writer, editor, publisher, and webmaster. When organic traffic plateaus, the culprit is rarely your writing—it’s usually technical friction hiding under the hood.

Because you don't have a development team, you need a lean, high-impact technical SEO audit checklist that targets the exact issues WordPress sites face. Here is how to audit your solo blog in an afternoon.

1. Fix Indexing and Crawlability

Google can't rank what it can't read.

  • Check your robots.txt: Go to `yourdomain.com/robots.txt` and ensure you aren’t accidentally blocking important directories.
  • Review your XML Sitemap: Install an SEO plugin (like Rank Math or Yoast) and submit your clean sitemap to Google Search Console. Ensure it only includes your actual blog posts and static pages—not messy author archives or attachment pages.
  • Check "Discourage search engines": Go to *Settings > Reading* in WordPress and make sure this box is unchecked. (It happens more often than you think after staging site migrations).

2. Streamline Your Plugin Stack

Plugins are the lifeblood of WordPress, but bloat destroys site speed.

  • Audit your plugins: Delete anything inactive. For active plugins, ask yourself: *Does this directly serve my readers or SEO?*
  • Test your database: Over time, WordPress databases collect digital debris like post revisions, spam comments, and transient options. Use a reputable tool like WP-Optimize to clean your database and keep query times fast.

3. Tame Core Web Vitals

As a solo blogger, you likely rely on shared hosting and a commercial theme. These can introduce heavy CSS and JavaScript.

  • Run PageSpeed Insights: Look at your mobile score first. If your Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) is over 2.5 seconds, take action.
  • Optimize Images: Never upload raw photos straight from your camera. Use a compression plugin to convert images to WebP format automatically upon upload.
  • Implement Caching: Use a caching plugin (or your host's built-in caching) to serve static HTML versions of your pages to visitors.

4. Clean Up Internal Architecture

Single-author blogs often suffer from orphaned content and bloated category structures.

  • Fix Broken Links: Use a tool like Screaming Frog (free for up to 500 URLs) or the "Broken Link Checker" plugin to find and fix 404 errors.
  • Audit Categories and Tags: If you have 50 posts spread across 40 categories, you are diluting your topical authority. Consolidate categories so your blog has a clear, hierarchical structure.

A technical audit isn’t a one-time event; aim to run through this checklist twice a year. By keeping your WordPress foundation clean, you ensure your content gets the visibility it deserves.
